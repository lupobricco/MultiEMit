\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental_design}

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{imgs/model_architecture.jpeg}
\caption{Dual-Encoder multimodal architecture. Text and image representations are extracted in parallel via domain-adapted BERT and ViT encoders, concatenated into a joint vector ($d=1536$), and passed to task-appropriate classification heads.}

This section details the experimental methodology established to evaluate affective classification on the MultiEMiT corpus across three core tasks: Emotion Detection (multi-label over ten candidate categories), Irony Detection (three mutually exclusive classes: \textit{Unironic}, \textit{Irony}, \textit{Sarcasm}), and Semiotic Relation Detection (\textit{Additive}, \textit{Parallel}, \textit{Divergent}). To ensure reproducibility and fair comparison, all datasets were split using a fixed 80\% training and 20\% test ratio (\texttt{random\_state=42}). Depending on the task, training and evaluation partitions were structured as follows: for Emotion and Irony Detection, text-only and multimodal baselines were trained by combining the textual dataset's training split with the multimodal training split (using strictly its text component). To expand training coverage and alleviate acute class imbalance on long-tail categories, the Emotion training split was augmented by concatenating the external EVALITA 2023 EMiT dataset \cite{emit}, while the Irony training split was augmented by concatenating the external IronITA dataset \cite{ironita2018}. Crucially, to prevent data leakage and guarantee an uncompromised in-domain evaluation, all test partitions consisted strictly and exclusively of MultiEMiT instances (zero contamination from external datasets). For Semiotic Relation Detection, because visual annotations exist exclusively for posts with paired media, training and evaluation were restricted entirely to the multimodal dataset's splits.

Because of severe class imbalance, raw accuracy provides a misleading indicator of model quality. We therefore center our evaluation on \textbf{Macro} and \textbf{Micro F1-scores}, selecting the aggregation scheme based on the analytical context. For global benchmarks across all tasks, \textbf{Macro F1} serves as the primary metric, weighting classes equally to penalize poor performance on minority labels. For fine-grained breakdowns across semiotic relations (Section~\ref{sec:results}), we report \textbf{Micro F1} to maintain statistical stability when evaluating smaller data subsets with sparse class distributions.

To establish strong text-only reference points, we evaluated models built on the RoBERTa architecture. For Irony Detection, we trained \textit{UmBERTo} \cite{musixmatch-2020-umberto} (pre-trained on Italian Common Crawl with whole-word masking) directly on the combined training set formed by concatenating MultiEMiT with the external IronITA dataset \cite{ironita2018}. For Emotion Detection, we leveraged transfer learning starting from an UmBERTo checkpoint already pre-trained on the \textit{FEEL-IT} affective corpus \cite{bianchi2021feel}, fine-tuning it on the combined training set formed by concatenating the MultiEMiT training split with the external EMiT dataset \cite{emit} to adapt its latent representation to our ten-class multi-label taxonomy. For Semiotic Relation Detection, UmBERTo was trained exclusively on the multimodal training partition. These text models process tweet text alone without visual inputs. To capture cross-modal interactions, we implemented a discriminative Dual-Encoder pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:dual_encoder_arch}). The textual modality is processed by the domain-adapted language encoder, while the visual modality $x_v$ is processed in parallel by a Vision Transformer (ViT-Base-224, specifically \texttt{google/vit-base-patch16-224}) $f_{\text{img}}$ \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit}, with input images resized to $224 \times 224$ pixels and normalized according to ImageNet statistics.
. The 768-dimensional \texttt{[CLS]} text embedding and 768-dimensional ViT pooled representation are concatenated into a 1,536-dimensional joint multimodal vector. To predict class labels from this representation, we employ task-appropriate linear heads: Scikit-learn's \texttt{ClassifierChain} wrapping an \texttt{SGDClassifier} for multi-label Emotion Detection to model label interdependencies, and standard multi-class logistic regression for the mutually exclusive Irony and Semiotic Relation benchmarks.

Motivated by strong cross-layer dependencies discovered during corpus analysis (Section~\ref{sec:dataset_analysis}), we developed a \textit{Correlation Prompting} strategy tailored to each model family. For discriminative encoders, because models undergo supervised training, they implicitly learn statistical co-occurrences when ground-truth auxiliary pragmatic labels are exposed in the input. For non-target dimensions, we added natural-language verbalizations of auxiliary annotations directly to the text (e.g., augmenting \textit{``ma soleil è con sua nonna?''} with \textit{``il seguente testo contiene ironia...''}). By contrast, when evaluating Pixtral 12B in zero-shot inference, the model cannot learn dataset-specific distributions from a single test instance. To bridge this gap, we explicitly inject empirical Pearson correlation rules derived from corpus analysis into system instructions prior to the tweet text (e.g., \textit{``Statistics: Sarcasm correlates positively with Disgust and Anger...''}). To measure prompt sensitivity, we designed five variants spanning three correlation intensity thresholds: \textbf{Short} ($|r| \ge 0.25$), \textbf{Medium} ($|r| \ge 0.20$), and three \textbf{Long} formulations (\textit{long\_v1}, \textit{long\_v2}, \textit{long\_v3}; $|r| \ge 0.15$). Following findings from the BRIGHTER benchmark \cite{brighter_project} showing that multilingual LLMs exhibit stronger instruction adherence when prompted in English, all generative instructions were formulated in English.

To compare specialized discriminative systems against modern generative vision--language models, we benchmarked \textbf{Pixtral 12B} \cite{pixtral}, an open-weights multimodal language model based on Mistral Nemo featuring a dedicated 400M-parameter vision encoder. For all Pixtral experiments, input images were standardized to a fixed resolution of $336 \times 336$ pixels via the model's image processor (\texttt{size = \{"height": 336, "width": 336\}}). We evaluated Pixtral across three experimental regimes: Zero-Shot Prompting across all five correlation variants and an unprompted baseline; Few-Shot (Three-Shot) Prompting for Semiotic Relation Detection, providing three complete multimodal exemplars within the prompt window; and LoRA Fine-Tuning on the MultiEMiT multimodal training set ($r=16$, $\alpha=32$, RSLoRA, all linear target modules, dropout $0.1$), adapting 66,322,432 parameters out of 12.75 billion (0.52\% trainable) with per-device batch size 1, 8 gradient accumulation steps, learning rate $3 \times 10^{-5}$, FP16 mixed precision, and gradient checkpointing over 2 epochs. To account for generation variance, each Pixtral configuration was evaluated across two decoding temperatures ($T=0.1$ and $T=0.9$) with 3 independent runs per setting (reporting mean scores). Finally, to address autoregressive generation anomalies during multi-label emotion fine-tuning—where ranking and exposure bias degraded the stopping token (\texttt{<EOS>}) and induced repetitive loops—we implemented a post-processing truncation parameter restricting extraction to the first $n=2$ unique valid taxonomy emotions, matching empirical ground-truth cardinality and eliminating unconstrained tail hallucinations.
