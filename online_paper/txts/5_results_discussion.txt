\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section presents the empirical evaluation of the unimodal and multimodal architectures across the three tasks: Emotion Detection, Irony Detection, and Semiotic Relation Detection. We analyze the quantitative impact of integrating visual features (comparing blind text baselines to the Dual-Encoder), the performance gains induced by correlation prompting, and the strengths and failure modes of Pixtral 12B. We also examine model behavior disaggregated across semiotic relational subsets.

\subsection{Emotion Detection}
\label{sec:emotion_results}

Emotion detection represents the most challenging benchmark due to the high cardinality of the ten-class label space and the multi-label nature of affective expression in social media. Exploratory experiments attempting to collapse co-occurring emotions into single-label multiclass dyads under Plutchik's taxonomy or Ekman's basic emotions suffered from acute class sparsity and severe majority-class collapse. Recasting the task as a 10-dimensional multi-label binary problem over Plutchik's primary emotions (plus \textit{Love} and \textit{Neutral}) resolved this structural bottleneck.

To address class skew on long-tail categories, we initialized our model via transfer learning from an UmBERTo checkpoint already pre-trained on the affective \textit{FEEL-IT} corpus \cite{bianchi2021feel}, and augmented its training split by concatenating the external EVALITA 2023 EMiT dataset \cite{emit}. Crucially, to prevent data leakage, all evaluation was conducted strictly and exclusively on the held-out MultiEMiT test splits.

Table~\ref{tab:emotion_baselines} summarizes the progression of our discriminative models across both evaluation partitions. On the purely textual test set, pre-adapting the affective latent space on the combined training set followed by retraining on MultiEMiT with correlation priors substantially lifts performance over the base text model, establishing our optimal textual baseline (Macro $F_1 = 0.590$).

When shifting to the multimodal test set, the blind textual baseline struggles when evaluated without visual context (Macro $F_1 = 0.300$). Introducing visual features via the Dual-Encoder architecture substantially enhances classification, lifting performance to a peak Macro $F_1$ of $0.450$ when combined with correlation prompting. This marked improvement (+15\% absolute over the blind text baseline) empirically confirms that visual context provides essential disambiguating cues for recognizing emotional expressions in social commentary, with correlation prompting providing a complementary boost.

During Pixtral fine-tuning, autoregressive decoding generated repetitive loops and extra-taxonomical tokens when predicting emotion lists. However, qualitative analysis revealed that predicted emotions were ordered by descending model confidence. Implementing the truncation parameter $n$ to retain the top-$n$ unique valid emotions effectively eliminated generative noise. As detailed in Table~\ref{table:truncation_parameter}, performance peaks distinctly at $n=2$ (Mean Macro $F_1 = 0.389$), whereas unconstrained generation degrades sharply as spurious tail predictions incur heavy false-positive penalties. Setting $n=2$ directly aligns with the empirical ground truth, where the vast majority of posts convey one or two concurrent emotions.

Disaggregating emotion detection across semiotic subsets (Table~\ref{tab:subset_emotion}) reveals a revealing architectural divergence between discriminative and generative paradigms:

For the \textbf{Dual-Encoder}, performance peaks on the \textit{Parallel} partition (reaching Micro $F_1 = 0.60$ with correlation prompting) and drops to its lowest on the \textit{Divergent} subset ($F_1 = 0.38$--$0.42$). Because the Dual-Encoder relies on linear feature concatenation, redundant text-image alignment in Parallel posts reinforces the latent signal. In contrast, the pragmatic tension in Divergent posts induces cross-modal interference (the modality gap), confounding linear classification heads.

Conversely, \textbf{Pixtral 12B in zero-shot inference} exhibits the inverse pattern, achieving its highest performance on the \textit{Divergent} partition ($F_1 = 0.44$) and its lowest on \textit{Parallel} posts ($F_1 = 0.23$). Because Divergent commentary predominantly consists of expressive reaction memes and satire (dominated by salient states like \textit{Joy}, \textit{Surprise}, and \textit{Disgust}), Pixtral's internet-scale pre-training allows it to naturally decode visual irony and satirical punchlines without task-specific training, even outperforming the unprompted Dual-Encoder ($0.44$ vs. $0.38$). On subtle, literal \textit{Parallel} posts, zero-shot Pixtral struggles due to a lack of domain priors, recovering only after supervised fine-tuning ($0.41$).

Across the dominant \textit{Additive} subset, correlation prompting consistently boosts accuracy for both architectures, confirming that statistical priors help models synthesize complementary cross-modal evidence.

\subsection{Irony Detection}
\label{sec:irony_results}

Irony detection presents an inverted dynamic compared to emotion recognition. In figurative discourse, visual content frequently introduces pragmatic incongruity rather than semantic convergence. 

To benchmark unimodal and multimodal discriminative classifiers, we trained UmBERTo directly on the combined training set formed by concatenating MultiEMiT with the external IronITA dataset \cite{ironita2018}, evaluating strictly on held-out MultiEMiT instances. Table~\ref{tab:irony_baselines} summarizes the progression of discriminative configurations across both evaluation sets.

On the textual test set, correlation prompting yields a substantial gain over the unprompted text baseline (reaching Macro $F_1 = 0.520$), confirming that cross-task priors guide figurative interpretation even in purely unimodal text.

When evaluated on the multimodal test set, the blind textual baseline achieves competitive results ($F_1 = 0.670$). In sharp contrast, incorporating visual features in the unprompted Dual-Encoder degrades performance ($F_1 = 0.630$), falling behind the text-only model. This degradation highlights a core multimodal challenge: unguided visual features act as semantic noise in figurative discourse. Because irony relies on intentional incongruity, raw visual embeddings pull the joint multimodal vector away from the textual intent. This pattern aligns with findings on the \textit{Modality Gap} \cite{liang2022mindgap}: concatenating representations from separately pre-trained encoders without joint cross-modal pre-training leaves a geometric misalignment in latent space.

Injecting statistical correlations reverses this drop: the correlation-prompted Dual-Encoder reaches the benchmark peak of \textbf{0.740}, outperforming both the blind textual baseline and unprompted multimodal fusion. Explicit priors enable the classifier to treat visual dissonance as evidence of irony rather than noise.

\subsection{Semiotic Relation Detection}
\label{sec:relation_results}

Semiotic relation detection requires the model to directly categorize the semantic interaction between modalities into Additive, Parallel, or Divergent. Because text-image relation annotations exist exclusively for posts with paired media, training and evaluation were restricted entirely to the multimodal partition of MultiEMiT.

Table~\ref{tab:relation_baselines} summarizes the performance of our discriminative baselines on this task. Even when evaluated on text alone with visual features masked, UmBERTo displays surprising baseline competence (Macro $F_1 = 0.610$, rising to $0.660$ with correlation prompting). This capability stems from subtle latent lexical cues: purely descriptive commentary strongly correlates with Parallel imagery, whereas commentary laden with background narrative expansion predominantly accompanies Additive imagery.

Introducing visual features via the Dual-Encoder directly confirms the benefit of multimodal modeling, reaching a benchmark peak of \textbf{0.690} when paired with correlation prompting.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While MultiEMiT establishes the first multi-layered multimodal benchmark for Italian Social TV, several methodological and resource constraints should be acknowledged:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset Scale and Class Sparsity:} Although the textual corpus comprises 2,594 posts, the multimodal partition contains 645 instances. This size reflects the substantial labor required for independent three-annotator adjudication across five pragmatic levels. Consequently, minority labels exhibit severe class sparsity (e.g., $N=4$ for \textit{Fear} and $N=20$ for \textit{Sarcasm}), which limits statistical power on extreme long-tail categories.
    \item \textbf{Domain and Genre Coverage:} Data collection was centered on two specific broadcasts representing political satire and reality competition. While this pairing provides contrasting discourse styles, viewer engagement dynamics in other television genres (such as live sports commentary, scripted drama, and breaking news) may present distinct multimodal patterns not fully captured here.
    \item \textbf{Subjectivity in Gold-Standard Consolidation:} Affective commentary on social media is inherently subjective. As demonstrated in Section~\ref{sec:dataset_analysis}, our majority-voting protocol (requiring agreement from at least two annotators) prunes plausible yet unaligned emotional readings in favor of a single consolidated target, potentially underrepresenting ambiguous blended states.
\end{itemize}
